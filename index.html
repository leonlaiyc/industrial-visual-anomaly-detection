Exploring PCB Anomaly Detection with CNN and YOLO

A Hands-on Computer Vision PoC

I wanted to understand how visual anomaly detection works by building a small, hands-on PoC. Using the public VisA PCB dataset, I prepared the data, explored normal-only CNN features, tested ROI scoring and robustness, and trained supervised YOLO models. This showcase brings together what I tried, what worked, what didn't, and what I learned.

THREE TECHNICAL EXPLORATIONS

Different questions. Different approaches.

These rounds belong together as a learning journey. PCB1, PCB2, PCB3 and PCB4 are separate board datasets, not a shared test set or one jointly retrained model.

APCB1

CNN Baseline

Can a model of normal appearance reveal an unfamiliar defect?

Normal images for fitting

  1. Data inspection
  2. Orientation & image alignment
  3. Pretrained WideResNet-50-2 features
  4. Per-position Gaussian / Mahalanobis distance
  5. Image anomaly score & heatmap

Orientation, registration and background interference shaped the baseline.

Learn the data before changing the model.

BPCB2 / PCB3

Improve and Stress-Test

Can preprocessing reduce noise without creating a new failure mode?

Separate PCB2 & PCB3 experiments

  1. CNN baseline
  2. Board ROI scoring
  3. Threshold calibration
  4. Controlled lighting correction experiment
  5. Robustness evaluation & engineering decision

PCB2 tested ROI scoring; PCB3 separately tested illumination correction.

A cleaner result needs a stress test.

CPCB4

Supervised YOLO

What changes when defect examples and annotations are available?

Defect annotations for training

  1. PCB4 defect annotations
  2. Mask-to-bounding-box conversion
  3. YOLOv8s / YOLO11s training

WHAT ACTUALLY HAPPENED

The useful part was in the failures.

A few representative results from frozen score records. Each finding stays attached to its own dataset and evaluation setting.

A

Model normal appearance first

Question
Could pretrained CNN features identify deviations without defect training labels?
Tried
Inspect and align PCB1 images, then fit a Gaussian at each feature-map position.
Observed
The original PCB1 baseline detected 72/100 defective test images (72% recall). Heatmaps and false alarms exposed alignment and background sensitivity.
Learned
The highest anomaly score can come from the background rather than the defect. Inspect the image and the scoring region together.
B

Improve, then challenge the improvement

Question
Would board-only scoring and lighting correction reduce false alarms?
Tried
Evaluate ROI scoring on PCB2; run a separate controlled correction experiment on PCB3, with calibrated thresholds and robustness checks.
Observed
PCB2: test-normal false alarms fell from 13% to 3%; recall stayed at 80%. PCB3: correction helped some lighting conditions but worsened false alarms under blur. The candidate was not adopted.
Learned
Fix the source of interference, then look for trade-offs. Improving one condition does not establish robustness.
C

Learn what supervision buys

Question
How would labelled defects change image-level detection?
Tried
Convert PCB4 masks to boxes and train YOLOv8s and YOLO11s, using one generic defect class and held-out defect folds.
Observed
Observed image-level recall: YOLOv8s 98% and YOLO11s 95%. Recall was higher in this supervised setting, but brightness confounding, weaker generalization to unseen defect types, and localization limitations remained.
Learned
An image flagged as defective does not mean its box precisely locates the defect. This exploratory study does not establish architecture superiority.

CNN fitting used normal images only. YOLO used defect annotations. Dataset, supervision and protocol differences prevent a controlled cross-round model ranking.

WHAT I LEARNED

Four lessons I would carry forward.

01

Data quality and preprocessing matter.

Check orientation, alignment, board masks and image conditions before trusting a score.

02

Investigate the root cause before increasing complexity.

False-alarm images helped identify background interference and motivated ROI scoring.

03

Better metrics can introduce unexpected trade-offs.

The PCB3 lighting correction helped some conditions and created a blur failure.

04

Model choice depends on data, labels and objectives.

Normal-only modelling and supervised detection answer different questions and require different evidence.
